Chapter 8 · Weeks 6–12

500–1,000: one format

1,000 is allowed to mean ~20 unprompted repliers, a weekly post that can clear four-figure views, and one sentence a stranger remembers. A badge from a follow-train does not count.

At 500 the temptation is to reinvent yourself. New aesthetic. New pillars. That is how 780 dies. The room finally knows what you are. Do not confuse them to feel fresh.

If a format dies three times, it is dead. Change the metric, not the entire persona. Three explainers that stall might mean they are too long. Cut them in half before you invent a newsletter.

This post took 5 minutes to write. Many others I labored over for an hour flopped. Ride a hot topic.

- Luckforest (@lubinho_k) https://twitter.com/lubinho_k/status/2058243579022323755

A live topic in your niche can give you the four-figure view post. Ride it only if you have an add. Do not build the whole account on waves. Waves do not love you back on Tuesday.

Stop introducing yourself. Stop “new here” posts. At 600 you are a peer. Write like one. The reply block does not end at 500.

Video is optional at this size. It pays off more from 1k to 10k. Below that, a tight written explainer with one screenshot usually beats an average video. If fear of being on camera is the blocker, do not stall on it. Written posts will carry you to 1,000.

When you are ready for video and demos

Video is a separate skill from writing, not just another format. X is pushing it and a face and voice create gravity that text cannot. Hallam’s public rules, which match what works for demos:

  • Raw beats polished. A phone and one light is the aesthetic. Overproduced founder video dies.
  • Lead with an emotion, not a feature. The first two seconds decide whether anyone stays.
  • Make a demo readable with the sound off: big visuals, short copy, the value visible immediately. Slow opens, tiny UI, clutter on screen and long captions get scrolled past.
  • A daily series compounds harder than one-off bangers. Use whatever gear you will actually use every day.
  • The real blocker is fear of the face and voice, not equipment. Start when written posts carry you past 1,000, not before.
A demo posted by an account with no friend network still launches with zero-follower physics, even at 2,000 followers. Seed replies from your Column C before you expect the demo to travel.

After 1,000: what changes

The rungs keep going. The public version of Hallam’s ladder, which he still treats as true in 2026, extends the three rungs in Chapter 2. Treat it as a preview, not a promise.

RungJobWhat fails here
1k to 10kGo viral on purposeRandom posting, no kill list, no sense of which wave to enter
10k+Become a brandInconsistent opinions, protecting ego instead of the brand

1k to 10k. Every post is still a test of format, angle and topic. When something hits, milk it. Changing too fast nukes the momentum. Keep the kill list and stop the formats that consistently die. Enter the conversation that is already happening with a specific take and a proof artifact, then attach your own story to it. Serialized experiments with stakes (“Day 40 of growing this to $X”) make people come back tomorrow. A one-off tip does not. Aim for customer viral (bookmarked, DMed, “how do I get this”) over vanity viral (quote-tweeted by people who will never buy).

10k+. Be predictable in kind, not in content: people should be able to guess the type of post. Turn milestones into callbacks (“a year ago I had zero, today…”), so old posts become inventory. Share opinions others can retell without you. Feature the builders in your bubble so you become the node. Show up in person and on live formats, because X is not the whole loop. Protect the brand: the mistakes that cost you the thing matter more once you have one.

Sell after the story, not before

Do not sell first. The story earns the right. Hallam’s public content mix from his 2025 thread is roughly 40% entertaining, 30% educational, 20% inspirational and 10% selling, and it only works because the other 90% made people care. Order matters: attention first, value second, then show what those two produced (sales, clients, a shipped thing). Opportunities such as sponsors, inbound clients and collaborations cannot be roadmapped. You stay visible long enough that they find you.

Hallam also claims free-tool SEO took a side project from zero to 700 to 800 clicks a day in three months. The idea: pick a tool you can ship in a day, put it on a real URL and capture search traffic. It is a distribution channel that does not depend on the X ranker. Self-reported, so test it on a small scale before you build around it.

Drill · 15 minutes

Write the one sentence a stranger should remember. Put it next to the last seven originals. Anything that does not serve that sentence gets a kill vote. You need three kills or the sentence is a slogan you are not running.
