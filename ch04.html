Chapter 4 · 28 min · Core skill · Do not skip

Reply craft

You can leave this chapter when you have a 40-account list split into three columns and can write the four reply shapes from memory.

Under 1,000 followers, originals are homework. Replies are distribution. A good reply is a mini-post parked on a page the model is already willing to show. You are not networking. You are publishing where distribution already exists.

1–3 posts a day and 50–100 replies the first 30 days.

- Daniel Smidstrup (@DanielSmidstrup) https://twitter.com/DanielSmidstrup/status/2066474894280274217

Keep the ratio, not the raw hours. ClimbX-speed is 50–100 replies and costs two to three hours. This course’s default is 15–25 good replies. Good means it passes the standalone test. Eighty “this!” replies is worse than twelve that could stand as posts.

Three columns, not one list

ColumnWhoJob
A · Peers25 accounts at ~100–2,500 in your nicheBecome a known name. They notice. They reply back. Most of the block, every day.
B · Borrow10 accounts at ~5k–80k who post dailyBorrow a room. Only with an add. First 15 minutes. 5–8 replies.
C · Reciprocals15 people who already replied to youGravity. Talk every week. Protect these names.

Split time 70 / 30: A and C first, B second. Big-account replies without an add are noise. Luckforest’s public recap is the warning: chasing heroes did not compound. Same-size tribe did.

I woke up to 1,000+ followers today. It took me 8 months and around 3,800 posts.

- Luckforest (@lubinho_k) https://twitter.com/lubinho_k/status/2104182801881006342

How to pick the 25 peers

They post about the thing you post about. They are close enough in size that a reply from you is socially cheap for them to answer. They are not a “connect?” account and not a news handle. Write the 25 names down. A list that lives in your head becomes “whoever is on For You,” which is how you waste a month.

Size of the post matters too. Reply where the author will notice you. A reply on a 40-like post from a founder with 800 followers is worth more than a reply on a 4,000-like post from a celebrity. The big account never sees you. The peer does, and their followers are your first distribution. Hallam calls this the “make friends” strategy, and says “reply guy” is the wrong name for it: you are not the person under every big tweet, you are finding your bubble.

Finding warm, small posts. In his one-to-about-198-follower experiment (two days, roughly 200 manual replies at the peak, no follow-for-follow), he used filtered feeds and search to surface posts small enough that the author would see the reply. Use search terms from your niche, open the repliers as well as the original poster, and sort by recent. Caveat he named himself: people found the account partly because they already knew his main account. Do not use 198 in two days as your forecast.

The standalone test

Before you tap Reply, ask: could this stand as its own post? If the sentence only works as “Great point!” it is cheerleading. Delete it. Cheerleading trains two graphs: theirs (you are a fan) and yours (you produce empty text).

Four shapes that pass

  1. Number + condition. “This works under 500 followers. Past that the same reply looks needy.”
  2. Specific add. One fact, one counter-example, one tool the author missed.
  3. Mini-story. Two sentences from this week, not a memoir.
  4. Precise question. A question only someone in the niche can answer. Not “thoughts?”

Fill-in versions to keep in a note:

Number + condition: This works until roughly [N]. After that [what changes], so [what to do instead]. Specific add: The part people skip: [missing step]. Without it, [consequence]. Mini-story: [Time] doing [thing]. First attempt: [result]. What changed: [one lesson]. Precise question: Would this still hold if [edge case]? That is the version I'd want to see.
Before and after Weak: “Love this, following your journey!” Stronger: “Ten years in a corporate job where marketing was someone else’s problem. First month doing it myself, every feature-list post died and the one explainer did not. Curious how you sequenced the story.”
Pass (illustrative) Author: “Replies don’t work anymore.” You: “They stopped working for me when I left the 200–2k peer set and only sat on 100k posts. Back on same-size accounts, 1 in 8 replies gets a profile visit.”
Fail “So true.” / “Let’s connect!” / a 180-word essay that ignores the post and pitches your app.

Volume, timing, 30-minute window

15–25 good replies a day is the default. Answer the author if they reply to you, 30 minutes, not tomorrow. Mutual-reply weight is one of the largest numbers in Chapter 1.

IST for English-speaking builders: peers late morning. Borrow-reach 8–10pm when US founders post. Friday can be a scoreboard original. Sunday is review, not a new persona.

What a reply is not

A chatbot paste. A quote-tweet dunk. A “let’s connect” under a stranger. A thread of seven compliments. A pitch on first contact. Those train the wrong graph and, at scale, look like the bots Phoenix is built to bury.

If you only have 45 minutes, do not stretch to 50 replies by lowering quality. Stay at 15–25 that pass the test. Quality means “would I publish this on my own profile.”

Drill · 40 minutes

Write Column A (25), B (10), C (start empty). Notifications on for five Column B accounts. Write four replies today, one of each shape, into the X log before you post them. Post them. Log whether anyone answered.
